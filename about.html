About Hrshop

An independent English-language editorial magazine about everyday workplace practices — hiring conversations, onboarding, feedback rituals, one-to-ones, meetings, remote-work rhythms and the small honest habits behind steady team work.

Hrshop is a magazine about working together. Nothing is sold here. Every article is free to read. No paid subscriptions, no courses, no coaching, no recruiting service, no software product, no affiliate links.

What Hrshop is

Hrshop is a slow editorial project about everyday workplace practices. We publish long, patient guides, essays and quiet reflections on the small ordinary work of working with other people — hiring, onboarding, feedback, one-to-ones, meetings, remote-work rhythms. Every article is free to read. There are no paid subscriptions, no gated content, no courses, no coaching services, no recruiting products, no HR software, no affiliate links, and no plans to introduce any.

What Hrshop is not

The name is our own editorial choice. Hrshop, in this context, is the title of this workplace-culture publication. If it reminds you of a real-world HR company, HR consultancy, HR software product, recruiting service, marketplace or other business that may share a similar name, we understand — but Hrshop as a publication is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to any such real-world business. We do not use their logos, materials, catalogue, or branding. Nothing on this site is theirs, and nothing they do is ours. Despite the name, Hrshop is not a shop — no products, no cart, no checkout, no marketplace.

What we cover

Six main sections and six subject categories run across the site. Together they cover the calmer end of workplace life: hiring conversations that respect the candidate; onboarding as a season, not a checklist; feedback that lands because it is specific and timely; one-to-ones that are worth the half hour; small team meetings that respect everyone in the room; the daily rhythm of remote and hybrid work; and the small honest habits that quietly hold a team together over months.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell subscriptions, memberships, courses, coaching, HR software, recruiting services or gated content. Everything here is free to read.
  • We don't publish affiliate content or product endorsements. There are no prices, shopping links, product SKUs or software recommendations anywhere on the site.
  • We don't offer legal, employment-law or professional HR advice. For questions about your specific situation, consult a qualified employment lawyer or HR professional in your jurisdiction.
  • We don't offer medical or mental-health advice. Anything that touches on workplace wellbeing points readers to a qualified professional.
  • We don't publish salary numbers, benchmarks, or specific pay data of any kind.
  • We don't publish political or religious content, or content about union politics.
  • We don't use real company names as case studies. All examples are anonymised or fictional composites.
  • We don't invent statistics, awards, university affiliations or reader numbers we can't back up.

How pieces are made

Each article is written by a named editor, edited for tone and structure, and reviewed before publication. If we get something wrong, we correct it in place and note the change at the foot of the piece. Our full approach is described in the editorial policy and corrections policy.

Where we are

The editorial office is in Bristol, UK. It is a working office, not a retail address; visits are by prior arrangement only. Reach us by email at editor@hrshop.com for general enquiries, pitch@hrshop.com for story pitches, and corrections@hrshop.com for corrections.

Meet the editorial team

Hrshop is written by a small editorial team of two fictional composite personas — created to hold a consistent editorial voice across the site. They are not real individuals. Their pieces are original editorial writing, not the professional opinions of any real person.

EW

Eleanor Whitcombe

Editor & workplace writer. Eleanor edits Hrshop and writes most of the hiring and onboarding pieces. She is interested in the ordinary Tuesday one-to-one and in workplace practices that do not require a training programme to justify themselves.

MT

Marcus Tremlett

Meetings & remote-work writer. Marcus writes the meetings and remote-work pieces. He is opinionated about agendas, patient about async communication, and gently sceptical of any productivity system that promises to solve a working week in a weekend.
